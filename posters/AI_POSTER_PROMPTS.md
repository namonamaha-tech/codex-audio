## PART A: The Design Guide (from a veteran poster designer's view)

### A1. What these posters must do
A desk poster is seen **hundreds of times from about 1 metre away**, usually in a 2–3 second glance between tasks. So each poster has to:
1. **Stop the eye in 1 second** with one huge, high-contrast idea (the headline or the verse).
2. **Deliver the message in 3 seconds**: headline plus one image that says the same thing without words.
3. **Reward a 30-second read**: 3–4 short rules and, on posters 1–2, the founder's credentials.
4. **Ask for a daily action**: a write-in line, so the poster becomes a tool rather than decoration.

### A2. Viewer psychology the design relies on
| Principle | What it means | How the posters use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Visual hierarchy / 3-level read** | The eye goes to the biggest, darkest thing first | Level 1: headline (largest). Level 2: illustration. Level 3: rules and stats. Never let two elements compete for first place |
| **Z-pattern scanning** | Western readers scan top-left → top-right → diagonal → bottom | Hook top-left, illustration across the middle, founder or action at the bottom right |
| **Figure–ground contrast** | Solid black against white grabs attention | One big black block per poster (the "SUCCESSFUL" panel, the road, the founder band) gives the page an anchor |
| **Von Restorff (isolation) effect** | The one thing that looks different is remembered | One reversed (white-on-black) highlight per section, e.g. "it's distraction." Use it rarely or it stops working |
| **Dual coding** | Words plus a matching picture are remembered far better | Bullseye vs scattered arrows · marathon road vs sprint · tree trunk vs bush |
| **Chunking (cognitive load)** | People hold about 3–4 items at once | 3 rules on Poster 1, 4 on Posters 2 and 3. Each rule is a 2–5 word title plus at most 2 lines |
| **Authority and social proof** | We believe advice from proven people | Posters 1–2 end with the founder's name, photo and hard numbers (net worth, company value, growth multiple) |
| **Faces attract attention** | Eyes go to a human face first | Founder photo (Posters 1–2), looking at the viewer, in high-contrast black and white |
| **Concrete numbers** | Specific figures feel true and stick | "829× in 28 years", "₹4,546 Cr", "25% a year for 25 years". Never "huge growth" |
| **Loss framing** | Avoiding a loss motivates more than a gain | "Those who were DISTRACTED", "many-branched and endless": the poster shows the cost of distraction |
| **Commitment and consistency** | Writing a goal down makes you more likely to act on it | Write-in lines: "MY ONE THING TODAY", "TODAY'S KILOMETRE", "MY ONE AIM" |
| **Whitespace = importance** | Crowded posters look like noise | 11 mm margins, at least 40% of the page left white |
| **Series recognition** | Repetition builds a "brand" in your space | Same grid, fonts, header strip and "DESK MANIFESTO Nº 0X" numbering on all three |

### A3. Visual style
**Swiss editorial × neo-brutalist × halftone print.** Bold condensed headlines, thick black rules, generous whitespace, halftone dot textures, outline numerals, registration-mark crosses, a small vertical label on the right edge. Modern, premium, "design-studio" feel, not a corporate slide.

### A4. Colour (for B&W printing)
- **Only pure black #000000 and pure white #FFFFFF.**
- Mid-tones come **only from halftone dots or hatching**, never from smooth grey gradients (laser printers turn those into muddy blotches).
- Photos: high-contrast grayscale (contrast +15%, slight sharpening).

### A5. Typography (all free, Google Fonts)
| Role | Font | Size on A4 |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | **Anton** (or Bebas Neue / Oswald Bold), ALL CAPS | 45–75 pt |
| Quotes and the human voice | **Playfair Display Bold Italic** | 13–18 pt |
| Body text | **Space Grotesk** (or Inter) | 9–10.5 pt, never under 8 pt |
| Labels, data, captions | **Space Mono** (or JetBrains Mono), caps, wide letter-spacing | 6–8 pt |
| Sanskrit (Poster 3) | **Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit** (or Noto Serif Devanagari) | 20–26 pt |

Use at most **4 typefaces**. Big numbers always go in the headline face.

### A6. Grid and format
- **A4 portrait, 210 × 297 mm.** Digital: **2480 × 3508 px at 300 dpi**. For a print shop add 3 mm bleed: 216 × 303 mm.
- Margins 11 mm (sides), 10 mm (top). 12-column grid, 4 mm gutters.
- Keep all text **at least 5 mm inside the trim**.
- Minimum line weight 0.3 mm; minimum text 6 pt (captions) and 8 pt (body).

### A7. Shared series elements
1. **Header strip** (top): thick 1.6 mm black rule, then "DESK MANIFESTO Nº 0X" on the left with "Nº 0X" in a black pill, and a short theme phrase on the right.
2. **Registration crosses** in both top corners.
3. **Vertical edge label** on the right margin (3–4 words, spaced mono caps).
4. **Outline numerals** (01, 02, 03) for the rules.
5. **One line icon** per rule (thin stroke).
6. **Write-in line** near the bottom.
7. **Founder band** (Posters 1–2 only): full-bleed black block at the bottom.

- **Reference:** Bhagavad Gītā, Chapter 2 (Sāṅkhya Yoga), Verse 41. Spoken by Shri Krishna to Arjuna.
- **Sanskrit (Devanagari), exactly:**
  > व्यवसायात्मिका बुद्धिरेकेह कुरुनन्दन ।
  > बहुशाखा ह्यनन्ताश्च बुद्धयोऽव्यवसायिनाम् ॥ २.४१ ॥
- **Transliteration (IAST):**
  > vyavasāyātmikā buddhir ekeha kuru-nandana

- **English translation (faithful to the Sanskrit):**
  > "O joy of the Kurus (Arjuna), on this path the resolute mind is one-pointed. But the minds of the irresolute are many-branched and endless."
- **Word by word:** vyavasāya-ātmikā: resolute, determined · buddhiḥ: mind, intellect · ekā: one, single-pointed · iha: here, on this path · kuru-nandana: joy of the Kurus (Arjuna) · bahu-śākhāḥ: many-branched · hi: indeed · anantāḥ: endless · ca: and · buddhayaḥ: minds · avyavasāyinām: of those without resolve.
- **Language note:** *vyavasāya* ("resolve") is also the everyday Hindi word for **business**.

## PART E: POSTER 3, "Bhagavad Gītā 2.41: One Aim"

> **Rule for this poster:** the content comes entirely from the Bhagavad Gītā, followed by a general founder's perspective for any startup. **No Dr. Velumani, no Thyrocare, no photo, no company names.**

### E1. Final text (copy exactly)
- Header: `DESK MANIFESTO` [`Nº 03`] · `BHAGAVAD GĪTĀ`
- **Śloka number (huge):** **2.41** with the label `BHAGAVAD GĪTĀ · CHAPTER 2 · VERSE 41`
- Ornament line: `SHRI KRISHNA TO ARJUNA · KURUKSHETRA` · ॐ · `SĀṄKHYA YOGA`
- **Śloka (Devanagari):**
  व्यवसायात्मिका बुद्धिरेकेह कुरुनन्दन ।
  बहुशाखा ह्यनन्ताश्च बुद्धयोऽव्यवसायिनाम् ॥ २.४१ ॥
- **Transliteration:** *vyavasāyātmikā buddhir ekeha kuru-nandana / bahu-śākhā hy anantāś ca buddhayo 'vyavasāyinām*
- **English translation:** "O joy of the Kurus (Arjuna), on this path the resolute mind is **one-pointed**. But the minds of the irresolute are **many-branched** and **endless**."
- Key words (small glossary): **vyavasāya**: firm resolve (in Hindi today: *business*) · **ekā**: one · **bahu-śākhā**: many-branched · **ananta**: endless · **avyavasāyin**: one without resolve

## PART F: Getting good results from AI tools

1. **Text is the weak spot of image AIs.** Generate in two passes:
   - **Pass 1:** the artwork only (split panel, road scene, tree vs bush) with **no text**. Add "no text, no letters" to the prompt.
   - **Pass 2:** put the artwork into a layout tool (Canva, Figma, Adobe Express, or the provided HTML files) and set the exact text in the fonts from A5.
   - ChatGPT image generation and Ideogram handle English text best. Still check every number (₹, ×, Cr) letter by letter.
2. **Sanskrit / Devanagari:** most AI models still garble Devanagari conjuncts (e.g. ्म, द्ध, ह्य). **Always paste the śloka as real text** in a layout tool, or ask the AI to leave a blank area for it.
3. **Aspect ratio:** ask for **1:1.414 (A4)**. If the tool only offers presets, choose **2:3**, then add white margin or crop to 210 × 297 mm. Upscale to **2480 × 3508 px** before printing.
4. **Founder photo (Posters 1–2):** upload an official press photo as a reference image ("use this person's likeness"). Use only photos you have the right to use.
5. **Higgsfield:** paste the **Full prompt** into an image model that supports long prompts and reference images. For tools with short prompt limits use the **Short prompt** plus the **Negative prompt**.
6. **ChatGPT:** paste the Full prompt and add: *"Render as a print-ready A4 poster. If any text would be distorted, leave that area blank instead."* Then ask for corrections one zone at a time ("fix the stat row only").
7. **B&W check before printing:** convert to grayscale, push contrast, and print one test page. Text should read clearly from 1 metre.
